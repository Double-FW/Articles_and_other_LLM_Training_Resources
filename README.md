# Articles and Other LLM Training Resources

This repository contains practitioner articles, governance frameworks and material intended for human use and AI-assisted retrieval. Documents have different authority types and must not be treated as interchangeable sources of law, standards requirements, organisational policy or research evidence.

## European Accessibility Act canonical route

Use the EAA material in this order:

1. [EAA Scope and Applicability Gate](EAA_Scope_and_Applicability_Gate.md) — Layer 1. Determine the subject, economic-operator role, jurisdiction, national law, dates, applicable Annex I route and separately recorded legal limitations.
2. [Inclusive Digital Service Outcome Dataset](https://github.com/Double-FW/Inclusive_and_Effective_Design/blob/main/Inclusive_Digital_Service_Outcome_Dataset.md) — Layer 2. Define and evaluate functional, task, equitable and experiential outcomes.
3. [EAA Evidence and Conformity Record Model](EAA_Evidence_and_Conformity_Record_Model.md) — Layer 3. Reach controlled requirement conclusions, govern claims and maintain monitoring.

The [Accessibility Test-Led Delivery Framework](https://github.com/Double-FW/QA_Accessibility_Testing/blob/main/Accessibility_Test_Led_Delivery_Framework.md) supplies generic requirements, tests, executions, issues, exceptions, baseline departures and release evidence.

The [Guide to the European Accessibility Act 2025](Guide_to_the_European_Accessibility_Act_2025.md) explains the context and reasoning. It does not replace the three operational layers.

## Authority boundaries

- Applicable national legislation and authoritative legal decisions determine binding duties in the relevant jurisdiction.
- Directive (EU) 2019/882 supplies the common EU framework but is not a complete national conclusion.
- Harmonised standards and common specifications have the legal effect available within their stated scope and current status.
- WCAG and other technical standards provide strong technical baselines and evidence; they do not establish EAA scope or complete service conformity by themselves.
- Inclusive-design outcomes cover broader functional and experiential quality; not every outcome is a direct statutory requirement.
- User research and operational evidence inform outcomes and can expose failures; they do not independently determine legal scope or erase an applicable requirement.

## Mandatory retrieval rules

An AI or autonomous system must:

1. retrieve Layer 1 before applying an EAA requirement or considering a compliance claim;
2. keep scope and legal limitations as separate fields;
3. preserve exact legal text separately from interpretation;
4. label every source by authority type, jurisdiction, version and date;
5. keep standards results, user outcomes, organisational-policy decisions and legal conclusions separate;
6. use Layer 2 outcome IDs when functional or experiential evidence is relevant;
7. use Layer 3 for requirement conclusions and every public claim;
8. return `legal review required` or `cannot conclude` when the record is insufficient;
9. prevent autonomous approval of scope, limitations, departures, conformity decisions or claims; and
10. verify current national law, official guidance and standards status before relying on time-sensitive material.

## Status and supersession

When a document declares itself superseded:

- do not retrieve its former content as active guidance;
- follow the named replacement;
- retain the superseded file only as a stable redirect and historical record; and
- do not combine obsolete and current rules into a composite answer.

Repository history preserves earlier versions. Current-file status, canonical routing and source authority determine active use.

## Human approval boundary

Repository material may support analysis and drafting. A named human authority must approve legal scope, statutory limitations, exceptions, alternative technical solutions, evidence-based departures, conformity conclusions and public claims.
