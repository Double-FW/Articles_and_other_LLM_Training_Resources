# Corporate Event Accessibility Guide  
  
Planning an accessible corporate event in requires moving beyond basic legal compliance to create an environment where all participants can engage equally.   

## Pre-Event & Registration

*   **Early Outreach:** Include a clear accessibility statement on all invitations, asking for specific dietary, mobility, sensory, or communication requirements.
*   **Accessible Registration:** Ensure your registration platform meets **WCAG 2.2 AA** standards and offer multiple registration methods (online, email, phone) to accommodate different needs.
*   **Information Sharing:** Send detailed "Joining Instructions" at least 14 days in advance. Include an **accessible map** showing step-free routes, quiet rooms, and accessible restrooms. The map should work without colour, be high contrast and come with a text only alternative that describes how to find things and how to request assistance.
*   **Timing:** Schedule events between **10:00 AM and 4:00 PM** to avoid peak travel and accommodate those with caring responsibilities.
*   **Communicate all of this to the attendees** and ask if they have any additional requirements, or if they have any considerations that they need to be checked, such as access to the stage, requirements for their support service dog to access water or particular needs for bathroom access or a personal emergency evacuation plan.

## Venue & Physical Access

*   **Entrances & Corridors:** Use venues with level, step-free access to all areas and door widths of at least **80–90cm**. Main aisles should be at least **1200mm wide** (ideally 1500mm) for wheelchair users to pass each other.
*   **Seating Layout:** Do not relegate wheelchair users to the back. Integrate accessible seating throughout the room, including near exits and the stage.
*   **Restrooms:** Ensure accessible toilets are unlocked, not used for storage, and preferably feature a **Radar Key** system or **Changing Places** facilities for high-dependency needs. Accessible bathrooms should support either left or right wheelchair transfer.
*   **Restorative Spaces:** Provide a dedicated **Quiet Room** for sensory regulation and another for prayer, or medication. These spaces should be low-sensory (soft lighting, minimal noise) and away from bathrooms, lifts or kitchens.

## Content & Communication

*   **Presentations:** Brief speakers to describe visual elements on slides where they represent key content.
*   Check all videos to ensure they are accurately captioned and also are fully understandable if you can only hear them. Make sure there are no videos or animations that contain flashing or flickering images,
*   At the beginning of all panel discussions ask each panellist to introduce themselves by name, what they do and what organisation they represent. This is so that anyone who cannot see them can identify their voice.
*   **Assistive Tech:** Provide **live captioning** and **Sign Language** interpreters as standard for large events. Ensure that anyone who needs these has access to seating near the front. Test hearing loops in all rooms, including break-out spaces.
*   **Digital Materials:** If the presenter agrees, share slides and also share agendas electronically in advance so attendees can use personal screen readers. To provide accessible slides or a transcript, presenters my charge a fee for their time to produce them. Be respectful as the content may be their intellectual property.
*   **Ensure all workshops and activities are accessible.** If in doubt, involve an event accessibility specialist. This includes ensuring digital as well as physical collaborative spaces and activities are accessible.
*   **If possible, have a camera focused on the head and shoulders of the speaker and screen above or on the stage.** This will help people who are lip readers.

## Catering & Hospitality

*   **Inclusive Menus:** Label all food clearly with ingredients and allergens. Offer vegan, gluten-free, halal, and kosher options as standard
*   **Accessible Setup:** Ensure buffet tables are at an accessible height (**71–86cm** / 28–34in) and have staff available to assist with carrying trays.
*   **Service Animals:** Ensure the venue has a designated relief area for service animals and provides water. Ensure this space is monitored, dog poo bags can be made available, and it is cleared up after the event.

## Staffing & Training

*   **Disability Awareness:** Train all on-site staff in **disability etiquette** and specific venue emergency procedures, including **Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs)**.
*   **Point of Contact:** Assign a dedicated "Accessibility Coordinator" or easily identifiable "Meeters and Greeters" to assist guests upon arrival. 

## Post-Event

*   **Feedback:** Use an accessible survey to specifically ask about the inclusivity of the event. Use this data to improve future programming.
*   **Archiving:** Share recorded sessions with **accurate transcripts** and captions for those who could not attend or need more time to process the information.
